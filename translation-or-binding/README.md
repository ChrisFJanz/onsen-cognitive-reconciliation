# Translation or binding

Explanatory material for draft-janz-onsen-cognitive-reconciliation and its
demonstrator: a concept note and two narrated videos.

The automated reconciliation process can be hard to follow from the demonstrator
alone. This material explains how the process handles the two cases in the
draft, TM Forum to L3SM and L3SM to L3NM, and where the handling differs. Of
greater interest, it explains why the handling must differ, under a framework
that distinguishes translation problems from binding problems.

Reconciling two models produces a set of correspondences. Where the two models
themselves hold the information that settles a correspondence, it is a
translation: it can be settled once and published as a mapping. Where only one
of the systems holds that information, it is a binding: it is made by a judgment
in that system, and no mapping can fix it in advance.

The distinction is not new in substance. Part IV of the note, "Antecedents",
shows where it already appears in the IETF's own documents (RFC 8309, RFC 9182
Section 5, and the TEAS TE and service mapping model, which already uses
"binding" in this sense) and in database research. The note consolidates and
names it.

The videos were revised on 2026-11-20. Both now place each kind of correspondence
on one map, which asks two questions of it: whether it concerns concepts or
individuals, and whether it can be settled from the models or only from the
systems. Matching the individuals of one system to those of another is
described as a binding.
